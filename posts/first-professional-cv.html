How to create your first professional CV

No formal employment yet? You can still write a clear, truthful CV that highlights education, skills, projects and community experience.

FutureReady Namibia editorial team•7 min read•Educational guide

What goes on a beginner CV?

Start with your name and reliable contact information. Add a brief profile, education, relevant skills, projects and any volunteering or extracurricular responsibilities. Include references only when appropriate and with permission.

Avoid sharing unnecessary sensitive personal information. Always follow the specific instructions in the job advertisement.

Make the opening profile useful

Rather than writing “hardworking person looking for any job”, describe what you can offer. For example: “Recent school leaver with basic spreadsheet and document skills, experience helping organise school activities, and an interest in office administration.”

Only claim skills you can demonstrate. Employers may ask for an example or practical task.

Experience does not only mean paid jobs

Relevant activities might include leading a school club, organising a fundraising event, helping at a family business or completing a personal project. Explain what you did and what you achieved without exaggeration.

You could write: “Created a spreadsheet to track expenses for a school event” or “Prepared promotional posters for a community project.” These statements are stronger when accompanied by evidence.

Presentation and proofreading

Keep layout consistent, use headings and plenty of white space, and choose a readable font. For an early-career candidate, one well-structured page is often a practical starting point, unless an employer requests something else.

Check spelling and dates carefully. Save a PDF for sharing where appropriate, and use a clear filename such as Name_Surname_CV.pdf.

Tailor each application

Read the job description. Highlight skills and examples that genuinely match the role, and write a brief accompanying message when requested. Save a copy of each version and note where you applied.

Our Career Toolkit includes a browser-based starter CV builder that you can fill in and print. It does not send your details to a server.

Key takeaway

A strong starter CV is easy to scan, specific to the opportunity and honest about what you have done.
